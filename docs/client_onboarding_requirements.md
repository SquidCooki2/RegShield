# Client Onboarding Requirements & Compliance Checklist

To ensure full compliance during the client onboarding process—particularly under SEC, FINRA, Reg BI, and standard KYC/AML regulations—wealth management firms (like Broker-Dealers and RIAs) must collect specific documents and information. 

This checklist aligns with the RegShield automated ingestion process, which checks these requirements to generate a green, yellow, or red status in the Compliance Dossier.

## 1. Identity Verification (KYC & CIP)
Under the Customer Identification Program (CIP) and Know Your Customer (KYC) rules, firms must verify the true identity of the client.

*   **Government-Issued Photo ID:** Driver's license or passport. (For Enhanced Due Diligence on High-Net-Worth or international clients, two forms of ID may be required).
*   **Social Security Number / Tax ID:** Required for tax reporting and CIP verification.
*   **Proof of Address:** Utility bill, bank statement, or mortgage statement (usually dated within 90 days). *Note: A P.O. Box alone does not satisfy CIP requirements.*
*   **Date of Birth:** Required to confirm legal age and for CIP verification.
*   **Citizenship and Residency Status:** Determines tax withholding (e.g., FATCA reporting) and eligible account types.

## 2. Anti-Money Laundering (AML) & Sanctions
Firms must ensure funds are not tied to illicit activities or sanctioned individuals.

*   **Source of Funds / Wealth:** Documentation showing where the money is coming from (e.g., sale of a business, inheritance, payroll).
*   **Beneficial Ownership Information (If applicable):** For entity accounts (Trusts, LLCs, Corporations), documents showing who ultimately owns or controls the entity (e.g., Trust Agreements, Articles of Incorporation).
*   **Sanctions / OFAC Screening:** Acknowledgment and verification that the client is not on any government watchlists.

## 3. Suitability & Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)
Under the SEC's Reg BI, broker-dealers must only recommend investments that are in the "best interest" of the retail customer at the time the recommendation is made.

*   **Risk Tolerance Questionnaire:** A scored assessment of the client’s comfort with market volatility, time horizon, and loss tolerance.
*   **Current Financial Statements / Net Worth:** Documentation of current assets (real estate, existing accounts) and liabilities (mortgages, loans). 
*   **Income Documentation:** Recent pay stubs or tax returns to determine suitability and financial planning projections.
*   **Existing Investment Account Statements:** Statements from competitor brokerages (e.g., if transferring assets via ACAT). These show current holdings, cost basis, and asset allocation prior to engagement.
*   **Investment Policy Statement (IPS):** Documents the client's objectives, constraints, and asset allocation targets.

## 4. Required Disclosures & Agreements
Clients must receive and acknowledge specific regulatory documents before accounts can be opened and funded.

*   **Form ADV (Part 2A & 2B):** For RIAs, a brochure detailing the firm's services, fees, conflicts of interest, and the specific advisor's background.
*   **Form CRS (Client Relationship Summary):** Required for retail investors; summarizes the types of services offered, fees, conflicts of interest, and legal/disciplinary history.
*   **Client Advisory/Account Agreement:** The legally binding contract outlining the relationship, fees, and services.
*   **Privacy Policy Statement:** Details how the client's data will be used and protected.

## 5. Vulnerable Adult Protection (FINRA Rule 2165)
To protect seniors and vulnerable adults from financial exploitation.

*   **Trusted Contact Person Form:** A document naming a trusted individual (age 18+) that the firm can contact if they suspect financial exploitation or have trouble reaching the client regarding health/capacity issues.
